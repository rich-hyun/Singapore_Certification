01

Executive summary

Our goal is reputable, national-level recognition from Singapore for the MST network — the base chain on which MUSTSEM issues dedicated enterprise networks, bridges assets and tokenises NFTs, SBTs and real-world assets.

Important: Singapore does not issue a single “blockchain certificate” for a chain. National-level recognition is built from a stack of government-backed schemes: cybersecurity certification by the Cyber Security Agency (CSA), data protection certification by IMDA, alignment with IMDA’s TradeTrust / OpenAttestation verifiable-document framework, and — most critically for MST — a clear regulatory position from the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS) on how MST, wMST and tokenised assets are classified.

Regulation

MAS position

MST is used as collateral, staked for rewards and bridged. A Singapore legal opinion on its classification comes first.

Security

CSA Cyber Trust mark

National cybersecurity certification (SS 712:2025). Our Vault / HSM / AES-256-GCM architecture is a strong starting point.

Data

IMDA DPTM

Data Protection Trustmark (SS 714:2025). Applies because the platform runs sign-up, dashboard and consultation forms.

Interoperability

TradeTrust alignment

Make SBT credentials and RWA records verifiable in Singapore’s OpenAttestation format.

04

Platform features

Services currently offered on the MST network.

Infrastructure

Dedicated enterprise networks

Each enterprise gets an independent, EVM-compatible chain with its own operating policy. Deposited MST is locked as collateral and a native coin is issued at a 1:1000 ratio; no unbacked issuance, and collateral cannot be reduced while the network runs. A governance token can be issued on request. Set-up target: within 24 hours of approval.

Infrastructure

Bridge

Moves assets between networks. Today: TMZ on JKK-TMZ is wrapped 1:1 into wTMZ, then a relay delivers wMST to the same address on the MST chain.

Bridge (JKK-TMZ) 0x41bf31f5cfa121deA3875e435382fd0a890ab938
Infrastructure

Staking

MST wraps 1:1 into wMST. Staked wMST earns MST rewards at 2% per year, claimable every 7 days, with no lock-up period.

wMST 0x3Dfc5a626a046CE0F01B130fA92dd4ef4A3f714B
Staking 0x8Fb01DA4FA713Ce590546dC440165170E02e7D07
Tokenisation

NFT

Issuance and ownership management of digital assets via API (POST /v1/nfts/mint).

Tokenisation

SBT (soul-bound tokens)

Non-transferable credentials permanently bound to the receiving wallet, with one designated guardian address — suited to certificates and qualifications.

SBT 0x58EA439b075e01E7a681b7798e3d8022e619eAD8
Tokenisation

RWA (real-world assets)

Original asset files (images, PDFs) are stored on IPFS and the resulting CID is written into an on-chain token for the recipient’s wallet.

RWA 0x44FDE9228ED16B64Af6C4Bf8f4f985079184c639
Protection

IPFS security registration for AI models

AI model files are AES-256 encrypted, stored on IPFS and their CID is time-stamped on-chain, so unauthorised modification can be detected and versions restored. Access is controlled per API key. Currently in pilot.

Developers

REST API & dashboard

Issue tokens and execute smart contracts through API calls (OpenAPI 3.1). Every response returns a transactionHash as on-chain evidence; status and logs are visible on the dashboard.

05

Security architecture

Controls already in place. These map directly to Cyber Trust and DPTM requirements.

Encryption

Sensitive data stored only as AES-256-GCM ciphertext, including data on IPFS.

Key management

HashiCorp Vault generates, stores and rotates keys; servers never hold plaintext keys. Encryption runs through the Vault Transit API.

HSM signing

Transactions are signed only inside an HSM (PKCS#11, CloudHSM); private keys never leave it.

Transport & requests

TLS 1.3, HMAC and JWT request authentication, idempotency keys, and HMAC-SHA256-signed webhooks.

Access control

Role-based access (operator, developer, verifier) under least privilege.

Audit & monitoring

Every request, signature and result is logged in a tamper-evident hash chain on WORM or S3-SSE storage, with 24/7 monitoring. An on-premises Vault/HSM option supports data-sovereignty needs.

06

Certification & recognition tracks

Each track is independent. Priority reflects legal risk, impact on credibility and how much it unlocks the others.

A

MAS regulatory position

Priority 1
Authority
Monetary Authority of Singapore
Law
Payment Services Act 2019 · Securities and Futures Act · FSMA 2022 Part 9 (in force since 30 Jun 2025)
Why
MST is locked as collateral, wrapped, bridged and staked for a 2% yield — features regulators look at when deciding whether a token is a digital payment token. Singapore-based firms providing digital token services need a licence even if they serve only overseas customers, and MAS has said it will generally not grant such overseas-only licences. RWA tokens may additionally be capital markets products.
  • Obtain a Singapore legal opinion classifying MST, wMST, enterprise native coins, NFTs, SBTs and RWA tokens
  • Review whether staking rewards and the bridge can be offered in or from Singapore, and to whom
  • Define RWA scope (record of an asset vs. ownership or investment rights)
  • If a licence is needed, assess the MAS FinTech Regulatory Sandbox
B

Singapore legal entity

Priority 1
Authority
ACRA
Why
CSA funding support is limited to Singapore-registered businesses. The entity structure also decides which licensing rules apply, so it should be designed together with track A.
  • Decide the Singapore role: operating company, R&D/technology subsidiary, or partner
  • Decide which entity holds MST treasury, staking reward wallet, bridge relayer and HSM keys
C

CSA Cyber Essentials → Cyber Trust mark

Priority 1
Authority
Cyber Security Agency of Singapore (CSA)
Standard
Cyber Trust (2025) = Singapore Standard SS 712:2025
Validity
Cyber Trust: 3 years with annual audits · Cyber Essentials: 2 years
Why
Singapore’s national cybersecurity certification, also recognised outside Singapore. Since April 2025 it covers cloud and AI security — relevant to our cloud HSM and AI-model registration service.
  • Complete the CSA self-assessment template against the controls in section 4
  • Add independent penetration test and written incident-response plan
  • Engage a CSA-appointed certification body
D

IMDA Data Protection Trustmark (DPTM)

Priority 2
Authority
IMDA · PDPC
Standard
SS 714:2025
Why
Voluntary, enterprise-wide certification of PDPA-compliant data handling, audited by IMDA-appointed certification bodies with annual surveillance audits. Applies because we collect account, contact and company data.
  • Keep personal data off-chain; store only hashes and CIDs on MST
  • Appoint a Data Protection Officer; publish English privacy policy and terms aligned with the PDPA
  • Document data retention and disposal periods
E

IMDA TradeTrust / OpenAttestation

Priority 2
Authority
IMDA · GovTech (open-source framework)
Type
Framework alignment — not a certificate
Why
TradeTrust is IMDA’s framework for verifiable documents, built on OpenAttestation and W3C Verifiable Credentials, with issuer identity resolved via DNS-TXT records. Our SBT credentials and RWA records are natural candidates.
  • Export SBT and RWA records as OpenAttestation-format documents
  • Publish a DNS-TXT issuer record on mustsem.co.kr
  • Build a custom verifier for chain 7601, or anchor document hashes to a public chain supported by standard verifiers
F

Independent smart-contract & bridge audit

Priority 1
Authority
Reputable third-party auditor
Why
Not a government scheme, but MAS counsel, certification bodies and partners will expect public audit reports. Bridges and staking contracts hold user funds and are the highest-risk components.
  • Audit wMST, Staking, Bridge, SBT and RWA contracts and the relay process
  • Verify published source code against deployed bytecode
  • Publish the reports on this page
